Learn

What this is

This is an educational game: you run a virtual UK limited company and keep its books properly. It produces fully correct, rules-abiding accounts — real double-entry bookkeeping, UK tax, statutory FRS 105 accounts as iXBRL — but it is a game: nothing is ever filed or sent anywhere. Data comes in only by CSV import; results leave only as documents you generate.

The six sections

Company
Who you are: name, registered number, financial year, directors & shareholders, and CSV import.
Sales
Money coming in: itemised invoices (with VAT and expense recharges), receipts, cash sales, credit notes.
Expenses
Money going out: supplier bills, payments, direct expenses, mileage.
Banking
Your accounts, transfers, interest & charges — and reconciling a bank statement against your books.
Pay Yourself
Salary (PAYE, NI, pension), dividends allocated by shareholding, and the director's loan account.
Company Tax
Corporation tax on adjusted profit, and (when VAT registered) the nine-box VAT return.
Accounting
The engine room: trial balance, P&L, balance sheet, journals (each explained), and year-end accounts as iXBRL.

Try this first — your first month

  1. Set your company up under Company · Details, then look at the directors & shares (you already own 100 shares).
  2. Raise an invoice to a customer, then record the receipt when they pay.
  3. Record an expense — then recharge it to a customer on a new invoice.
  4. Run a salary for yourself and declare a dividend.
  5. Open the Journals and expand one — see exactly what each debit and credit does.
  6. Generate your year-end accounts as an iXBRL document, then close the year.

Key terms

Double-entry
Every transaction has two equal sides — a debit and a credit — so the books always balance.
Debit / credit
The two sides of a posting. Assets and expenses go up on the debit side; liabilities, income and equity go up on the credit side.
Ledger
The complete record of every journal. Control accounts (like trade debtors) summarise the subsidiary ledgers behind them.
VAT
Value Added Tax: you charge output VAT on sales and reclaim input VAT on purchases; the difference is the VAT return.
Corporation tax
Tax on the company's profit, after adjusting accounting profit for tax (adding back depreciation, deducting capital allowances).
Dividend
A share of profit paid to shareholders — only out of distributable reserves, and split by shareholding.
Retained earnings
Accumulated profit kept in the company. The year-end close moves each year's profit here.
Reconciliation
Checking your books against an external record — like a bank statement — until they agree.
iXBRL
Inline XBRL: accounts that read normally in a browser but carry machine-readable tags — the format filed at Companies House.
